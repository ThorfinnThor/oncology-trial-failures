# Classification Audit — Batch 18

This section is the completed manual semantic audit for records 3401–3600 and uses the same methodology and taxonomy as Batches 1–17.

Batch boundary: record 3401 is `NCT02985021`; record 3600 is `NCT00066677`.

The consistency rules from the preceding batches are applied strictly. Decision/action wording without a causal explanation remains `OTHER/UNKNOWN`; concrete strategy, portfolio, funding/support, supply, staffing, recruitment, feasibility, protocol/design, resource, and treatment-landscape causes are `OPERATIONAL`; explicit negative efficacy or tolerability/safety evidence takes biological precedence; direct regulator/IRB/ethics actions and explicit clinical holds are `REGULATORY`; and review, benefit-risk, milestone, transition, or mixed-cause wording is not forced into a single label without sufficient polarity, dimension, and causality.

## Main finding

Batch 18 still concentrates most definite error at the `OPERATIONAL` versus `OTHER/UNKNOWN` boundary, but less overwhelmingly than Batch 17. Of the 63 definite errors, 36 are `OTHER/UNKNOWN` records with a concrete operational cause and 12 are labeled `OPERATIONAL` despite supplying only a decision/action without the underlying cause. Together these 48 records account for 76.2% of all definite Batch 18 errors.

The remaining 15 definite errors are 11 missed `EFFICACY/FUTILITY` cases, two missed `SAFETY` cases, and two missed `REGULATORY` cases. Batch 18 contains no inverse biological false positive. The batch further sharpens several boundaries: concrete strategy/business reasons remain distinct from bare decisions; treatment-landscape, eligibility, drug-expiry, protocol-design, competing-study, and staffing causes remain undercovered; direct negative efficacy semantics continue to leak; and high-confidence operational labels are still assigned to content-free decision/action text.

## Classifier issues reinforced or newly exposed by Batch 18

1. **Decision/action false positives remain structural.** `Business decision`, `Sponsor decision`, `Pharmaceutical company decision`, and a bare statement that enrollment is being halted still become HIGH-confidence `OPERATIONAL` without a causal layer.
2. **Concrete business/strategy reasons remain underrecognized.** `Business reasons`, `Strategic considerations`, strategy changes/adjustments, R&D strategy changes, de-prioritization, program-scope reduction, and resource refocusing are operational when they actually explain the stop; they must stay separate from a bare business/strategic decision.
3. **Operational vocabulary remains incomplete beyond ordinary accrual terms.** Batch 18 misses competing studies, PI departure, protocol violations, medication expiry/logistics, treatment-landscape changes, standard-of-care change, study-design change, eligibility/screen failures, and explicit feasibility language.
4. **Misspellings and lexical variants still matter.** `recrual`, `accural`, and `futilty` are semantically clear in context and should not fall through to `OTHER/UNKNOWN`.
5. **Treatment-landscape causality needs stronger operational coverage.** Another efficacious therapy, changing efficacy of available treatments, changed standard of care, and a changed prostate-cancer treatment landscape can make a study obsolete or infeasible without representing a failure of the study intervention.
6. **Direct negative efficacy semantics continue to leak.** Lack of effect, no significant treatment-arm difference with futility judgment, no evidence of promise, a primary endpoint that can no longer be reached, lack of survival benefit, no objective responses, explicit futility, effect below expectation, and insufficient clinical benefit all require efficacy/futility coverage.
7. **Direct safety evidence can still remain unknown.** A treatment-related death and a direct stop reason of `Safety` should map to `SAFETY`.
8. **Explicit clinical holds remain regulatory even when another issue triggered the hold.** A partial clinical hold and an FDA hold are direct external oversight actions; an underlying manufacturing issue does not erase the regulator-imposed stop mechanism.
9. **Interim/monitoring/external-authority language still requires polarity, dimension, and causality gating.** IDMC recommendation, interim analysis, FDA feedback, a negative external-study result, or an overall benefit-risk assessment cannot be forced to a biological or regulatory class without the missing semantic layer.
10. **Mixed causes remain irreducible under keyword precedence.** Batch 18 contains operational+efficacy, safety+PK/formulation, safety+efficacy, and operational+efficacy+safety combinations that require review or multi-cause representation.
11. **Milestones and availability transitions need non-failure handling.** Enrollment completed, study completed, data collection/analysis completed, commercial availability, and an EMA waiver are not automatically failure causes.
12. **Conditional/stopping-rule language should use the scientific dimension, not only trigger words.** A prespecified two-stage rule based on lack of promise or response is efficacy/futility, while a bare `interim analysis` or committee recommendation still needs the adverse dimension.
13. **Confidence remains poorly calibrated.** Bare decisions/actions can receive HIGH-confidence `OPERATIONAL`, while explicit business/strategy, feasibility, efficacy, safety, and regulatory causes remain LOW-confidence `OTHER/UNKNOWN`.
