How to judge a case
  • Judge only what the stop reason states. Don't infer a cause from the drug, the sponsor or what you know about the programme.
  • A negated cause does not count: “not due to safety concerns” is not a safety stop.
  • Only a stated result counts. “Possible lack of efficacy” or “after an interim analysis” without the result is Unclear.
  • “Sponsor decision” with no reason is Decision without a stated cause. “Business” or “strategic reasons” is Not about the drug.
  • A regulatory hold with a stated toxicity is Biological failure → Safety. A hold without a medical cause is Not about the drug → Regulatory.
  • Two or more causes from different areas (e.g. futility and slow accrual) are Several causes.
  • “Futility” that the text ties only to slow or insufficient enrolment is Not about the drug → Recruitment: the trial could not answer its question, it did not answer it negatively.
  • “Nobody enrolled” and nothing more (“0 participant accrual”, “no participants enrolled”) is Not a failure → Never started: it restates that the trial enrolled no one and gives no cause. Choose Not about the drug → Recruitment only when the text says enrolment was attempted and failed (“unable to recruit”, “no accrual in one year”, “no patients after the study opened”). If the text names any cause, that cause decides even when nobody was enrolled: “withdrawn due to low accrual”, “closed per low accrual policy”, “lack of interest” are Recruitment; “no funding” is Funding.
  • The sponsor is who decided, not a cause. A sponsor stopping for a business, portfolio or development reason (“to focus on another route of administration”, “portfolio prioritisation”) is Not about the drug → Sponsor's business / strategy decision. “Sponsor decision” with no reason is Decision without a stated cause, never Business. Who decided is not a second cause: “terminated by the sponsor due to inferior efficacy” is Biological failure → Efficacy, not Several causes. Partner or funder withdrew support is for someone other than the sponsor pulling out: a collaborator, a funder, a drug supplier.
  • A new standard of care, a new approval or a changed treatment landscape that makes the study obsolete (“the research treatment is no longer the best option”) is Not about the drug → Protocol / feasibility, not a business decision. If the text says the change stopped enrolment (“impacted recruitment”), choose Recruitment. It is Sponsor's business / strategy decision only when a business reason is given (competition, strategy, portfolio).
  • A recall or removal of product lots (contamination, a quality defect, “for safety reasons” about the batch) is Not about the drug → Supply / manufacturing. Safety means harm from the drug's effect in people.
  • Cases with SEC filings: first say whether the filing is about this trial, about the drug's programme (which this trial was part of), or about another trial or drug. Label the cause the filing states, as for a registry text: a programme ended because the drug failed in another trial is Biological failure → Efficacy (as “withdrawn based on new efficacy data from another study” already is); “pipeline prioritisation” is Sponsor's business / strategy decision. Step 0 records that it came from the programme, so programme stops can be counted apart. If the filing is about another trial or drug, judge the registry text alone.
  • The trial details are there for orientation. When the text and the details disagree, judge the text.
  • In batches marked “confirm”, our label is shown: press Correct if it fits the text, otherwise Not correct and choose the right one. About one label in twenty there is deliberately wrong, to check attention.
  • Work in as many sittings as you like. Every answer is saved the moment you press Save; next time you start at the first open case. “Later” leaves a case open without answering it.
